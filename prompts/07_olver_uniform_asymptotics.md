## Governing equation and verified notation

Start from the verified Whittaker–Ince form

\[
z^2\Psi''+(B_1+B_2z)\Psi'+(B_3+qz)\Psi=0,\qquad q\ne0,
\]

where

\[
B_1=\frac{2\overline F}{V},\qquad B_2=\frac{2\hbar\omega_0}{V},\qquad B_3=-\frac{2E}{V},\qquad q=\frac{2F}{V}.
\]

Use the Stage 06 abbreviations

\[
D=B_3+\frac{B_2}{2}-\frac{B_2^2}{4},\qquad H=B_1\left(1-\frac{B_2}{2}\right),\qquad C=4D-\frac34.
\]

Fix a square root \(s^2=-q\), \(s\ne0\), and write

\[
a_\sigma=2\sigma s,\qquad \sigma\in\{+1,-1\}.
\]

## Part A — Remove the ramification exactly

Pass to the quadratic covering

\[
z=t^2,
\]

using compatible branches, and derive the transformed equation from the current manuscript rather than quoting it. Starting either from the exact Liouville normal form or directly from the equation for \(Y(t)=\Psi(t^2)\), verify the exact dependent-variable transformation

\[
\Psi(t^2)=t^{1/2-B_2}\exp\!\left(\frac{B_1}{2t^2}\right)w(t)
\]

and verify that \(w\) satisfies

\[
w''(t)={a_\sigma^2+g(t)}w(t),
\]

where the right-hand side is independent of \(\sigma\) because \(a_\sigma^2=-4q\), and

\[
\boxed{g(t)=-\frac{C}{t^2}-\frac{4H}{t^4}+\frac{B_1^2}{t^6}.}
\]

Check every sign. In particular, independently substitute the transformation back into the original equation. Explain that infinity, formerly a ramified rank-(1/2) point in \(z\), has become an unramified rank-1 point in \(t\), and that \(g(t)=O(t^{-2})\) is integrable along rays to infinity.

## Part B — Construct actual sectorial solutions

For each \(\sigma\), seek

\[
w_\sigma(t)=e^{a_\sigma t}{1+\varepsilon_\sigma(t)}.
\]

Derive the corresponding Volterra integral equation. Do not merely cite a general theorem. One acceptable route is variation of constants with the boundary conditions

\[
\varepsilon_\sigma(t)\to0,\qquad \varepsilon_\sigma'(t)\to0
\]

along an admissible path tending to infinity. Derive the integral kernel explicitly and check it by differentiation.

### Admissible/progressive paths

Define the domain geometrically. A path \(\Gamma_\sigma(t)\) from \(t\) to infinity is admissible if it remains in the selected branch domain, avoids \(t=0\), and is progressive for the exponential phase: parameterized toward infinity, \(\Re[a_\sigma(u-t)]\) must be nondecreasing (or impose the exactly equivalent condition required by the derived kernel). Do not hide this condition behind terminology.

State a concrete sector version. For example, on every closed subsector on which a ray to infinity satisfies the progressive condition and \(|t|\ge T>0\), the construction is uniform. Treat anti-Stokes boundary rays carefully: include them only if the kernel estimate remains valid there; otherwise work on closed proper subsectors and say so.

## Part C — Prove an explicit remainder bound

Define a computable variation/error-control quantity, for example

\[
G_\sigma(t)=\frac{1}{2|a_\sigma|}\int_{\Gamma_\sigma(t)}|g(u)|\,|du|,
\]

or the sharper quantity naturally produced by the verified kernel. Prove a bound of the form

\[
|\varepsilon_\sigma(t)|\le \exp{G_\sigma(t)}-1,
\]

with any necessary kernel constant displayed explicitly. Do not assert this exact formula if the derivation gives a different constant or norm.

For a radial admissible path \(u=re^{i\theta}\), derive a fully explicit majorant from

\[
|g(u)|\le\frac{|C|}{r^2}+\frac{4|H|}{r^4}+\frac{|B_1|^2}{r^6}.
\]

The final manuscript should contain an explicit expression such as

\[
\int_{|t|}^{\infty}|g(re^{i\theta})|\,dr\le\frac{|C|}{|t|}+\frac{4|H|}{3|t|^3}+\frac{|B_1|^2}{5|t|^5},
\]

and the resulting uniform \(O(|t|^{-1})=O(|z|^{-1/2})\) relative-error estimate. Also derive a bound for the derivative if it follows cleanly from the same integral equation.

If the elementary Volterra kernel does not control one of the exponential branches on the same sector, state separate progressive sectors/paths for the two signs. Do not claim simultaneous control where the proof does not provide it.

## Part D — Match the formal coefficient

Go one step beyond a bare \(O(t^{-1})\) theorem. Insert

\[
w_\sigma(t)=e^{a_\sigma t}\left(1+\frac{v_1^{(\sigma)}}{t}+O(t^{-2})\right)
\]

into the exact normal-form equation and derive

\[
v_1^{(\sigma)}=\frac{C}{2a_\sigma}
\]

after checking the sign against the verified equation. Then undo the exact transformation

\[
\Psi(t^2)=t^{1/2-B_2}e^{B_1/(2t^2)}w(t)
\]

and verify that this is exactly the Stage 05 coefficient

\[
u_1^{(\sigma)}=\frac{C}{2a_\sigma}.
\]

Strengthen the theorem to

\[
w_\sigma(t)=e^{a_\sigma t}\left(1+\frac{C}{2a_\sigma t}+\rho_{1,\sigma}(t)\right),\qquad \rho_{1,\sigma}(t)=O(t^{-2}),
\]

uniformly on the stated closed subsectors, if this can be proved in the same stage by substituting the one-term truncation and applying the Volterra bound to its residual. Give an explicit bound or error-control integral for \(\rho_{1,\sigma}\). If a clean rigorous proof cannot be completed, retain the proved \(O(t^{-1})\) theorem and label the coefficient matching as formal; do not blur the distinction.

Translate the result to the original variable:

\[
\Psi_\sigma(z)=e^{2\sigma\sqrt{-qz}}z^{1/4-B_2/2}\left[1+u_1^{(\sigma)}z^{-1/2}+O(z^{-1})\right],
\]

with the branch conventions and uniform sector stated explicitly.

## Part E — Stokes geometry of the exponential phases

From the two phase functions

\[
S_\sigma(z)=2\sigma\sqrt{-qz},
\]

derive the two geometrically invariant conditions

\[
\Re\sqrt{-qz}=0,\qquad \Im\sqrt{-qz}=0.
\]

Explain which condition gives equal moduli of the two exponentials and which gives maximal exponential separation. Because the names “Stokes line” and “anti-Stokes line” are interchanged in parts of the literature, state the paper's convention explicitly instead of relying on the names alone.

For \(q=|q|e^{i\phi}\) and \(z=re^{i\theta}\), derive the corresponding ray angles, with all branch and modulo-\(2\pi\) conventions checked. Also give the simpler covering-plane description

\[
\Re(st)=0,\qquad \Im(st)=0,\qquad s^2=-q,
\]

and explain how \(z=t^2\) maps and identifies the relevant rays. Distinguish this phase geometry from the still-uncomputed Stokes multipliers: locating the rays does not compute the switching constants.

## Part F — Relation to Olver, spectral role, and limitations

Identify the precise theorem, chapter, or error-control framework in Olver that the argument instantiates. Verify bibliographic details from the repository's cited edition before giving section or theorem numbers. If the exact numbering cannot be verified, cite the chapter/topic without inventing a theorem number.

State explicitly what has and has not been established:

Established:

- actual solutions, not merely formal series;

- their uniqueness under the specified asymptotic normalization;

- their uniformity on the stated domains;

- an explicit error-control quantity and remainder bound;

- agreement of the first coefficient with the direct Stage 05 recurrence, to exactly the rigor level proved in Part D;

- the Stokes/equal-modulus and maximal-separation rays determined by the leading exponential phases, but not their multipliers.

Not established:

- continuation through Stokes switching;

- Stokes multipliers or connection coefficients;

- matching to the solution analytic at \(z=0\);

- the global Bargmann spectral condition or a characteristic determinant;

- asymptotic eigenvalue formulas and their error bounds;

- any large-parameter asymptotics of the spectral condition;

- turning-point uniformization if finite zeros of the exact WKB momentum are discussed.

Add a short but essential spectral clarification. The label “dominant” refers only to relative size of \(e^{\pm2\sqrt{-qz}}\) inside a sector. Since these solutions have growth of order \(\exp(O(|z|^{1/2}))\), do not claim without a separate global proof that the dominant branch is excluded by the Gaussian Bargmann norm. In particular, do not manufacture a quantization rule by simply setting the coefficient of that branch to zero.

At the same time, do not suggest that continued fractions are logically required. Record the intended Olver-based spectral programme in conditional, mathematically precise form: construct a canonical solution selected by admissibility at the irregular point \(z=0\) (absence of the essential singular component and actual analyticity/entireness, not merely a formal Taylor series); connect it to canonical solutions at infinity; impose the global Bargmann growth condition; and express compatibility as the vanishing of a suitably normalized Wronskian or connection coefficient

\[
D(E)=0.
\]

Do not assert in Stage 07 that such a \(D(E)\) has already been constructed, that one infinity branch is automatically “bad”, or that its zeros have already been approximated. State that a later Olver analysis of the connection problem may yield asymptotic eigenvalue equations and error bounds. Do not speculate there about large energy, large level number, or small \(V\) as alternative asymptotic regimes. If a large parameter eventually becomes mathematically necessary, the only admissible choice is \(|F|/V\), and it must be introduced at the latest possible point rather than in the present local construction. Before any later stage uses a Taylor solution at \(z=0\), it must distinguish rigorously between a formal series, a convergent local analytic solution, and an entire Bargmann function; generic convergence must not be assumed.

Explain that the present covering-space normal form has no turning point near infinity because its leading coefficient is the nonzero constant \(-4q\). Finite turning points remain relevant to global continuation, not to this asymptotic-existence theorem at sufficiently large \(|t|\).

End with a short transition sentence: the next stage will remain within Olver's framework and ask how these normalized sectorial solutions connect to the admissible solution at the origin and how the resulting connection coefficient defines a spectral condition. Do not assign a separate named framework to a future quasiclassical stage.
