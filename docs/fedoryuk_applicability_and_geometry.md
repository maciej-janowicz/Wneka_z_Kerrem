# Fedoryuk applicability and geometry for the driven Kerr equation

## Conclusion

The strongest justified statement is **Outcome A: a verified sectorial
Fedoryuk applicability framework**.  The exact normal form satisfies the
local analytic prerequisites for ordinary sectorial WKB/Fedoryuk
constructions on a cut canonical domain which avoids the turning points and
the pole, provided that the required progressive paths are separately
verified on that domain.  Simple-turning-point and close/double-turning-point
local models are identified.  The complete required collection of canonical
domains and progressive paths has not been explicitly constructed.  No
checked result therefore supplies the global chain, its sheet and gauge
monodromy, or its identification with the Bargmann-entire germ; neither a
global connection formula nor an asymptotic spectral condition is proved.

This approach starts from the Bargmann equation and does not use the Olver
covering equation as input.

## Direct normal form

After the unitary rotation generated by the number operator, take
`F=|F|>0`.  Put

\[
 \eta=|F|/V,
 \qquad \delta=\hbar\omega _0/V,
 \qquad {\cal E}=2E/V.
\]

Multiplication of the exact Bargmann equation by `2/V` gives

\[
 z^2\Psi''+(2\delta z+2\eta)\Psi'
 +(2\eta z-{\cal E})\Psi=0.                 \tag{1}
\]

After division by `z^2`, write the first-derivative coefficient as

\[
 A(z)=\frac{2\delta}{z}+\frac{2\eta}{z^2}.
\]

The exact Liouville gauge

\[
 \Psi(z)=\exp\!\left[-\frac12\int A(z)\,dz\right]u(z)
 =z^{-\delta}e^{\eta/z}u(z)                 \tag{2}
\]

is understood on a fixed punctured sector with a fixed logarithm.  It removes
the first derivative and gives

\[
 u_{zz}=R(z)u,
\quad R=\frac{\eta^2}{z^4}
 +\frac{2\eta(\delta-1)}{z^3}
 +\frac{{\cal E}+\delta^2-\delta}{z^2}
 -\frac{2\eta}{z}.                          \tag{3}
\]

Now set

\[
 z=\eta^{1/3}y,\qquad {\cal E}=2\eta^{4/3}e,
 \qquad \varepsilon=\eta^{-2/3}.
\]

Since `d^2/dz^2=eta^{-2/3}d^2/dy^2`, equation (3) is exactly

\[
 \varepsilon^2u_{yy}=Q(y,e,\varepsilon)u,    \tag{4}
\]
\[
 Q=Q_0+\varepsilon Q_1+\varepsilon^2Q_2,
\quad Q_0=\frac1{y^4}+\frac{2e}{y^2}-\frac2y,
\quad Q_1=\frac{2(\delta-1)}{y^3},
\quad Q_2=\frac{\delta(\delta-1)}{y^2}.      \tag{5}
\]

There are no omitted higher powers: (5) is the exact transformed equation
when `e` denotes the exactly scaled energy.  In particular,

\[
 Q_0(y,e)=\frac{P(y;e)}{y^4},\qquad
 P(y;e)=1+2ey^2-2y^3.                        \tag{6}
\]

Thus the small parameter of the direct Fedoryuk equation is
`epsilon=eta^(-2/3)`, not `eta^(-1/3)`.  The latter is the displacement
expansion parameter used in the independent oscillator calculation; its
square is the WKB parameter in (4).

### Transformation ledger

* The phase rotation making `F` positive is unitary and introduces no
  singularity.
* Division by `z^2` exposes the genuine singular point `z=0` of (1).
* The factor `z^{-delta}` introduces a branch only because the Liouville
  gauge is used on a punctured sector.  The original physical solution is
  single-valued; the gauge monodromy must cancel in the pullback.
* The factor `exp(eta/z)` exposes one of the two formal exponential scales at
  the genuine irregular singularity.  It is not an extra singularity of the
  original equation.
* The dilation `z=eta^(1/3)y` is one-to-one and creates no branch points.
* `p=sqrt(Q0)` introduces the two sheets of the momentum surface and branch
  points at the zeros of `P`; these are WKB turning points, not singularities
  of (1).

## Singularities and turning points

For finite nonzero `epsilon`, (4) has one finite singular point, `y=0`, where
`Q` has a fourth-order pole (the lower-order terms have poles of orders three
and two).  This is the image of the genuine rank-one irregular point `z=0`.
Every nonzero zero of `Q` is an ordinary point of the differential equation,
although it is a turning point of the chosen WKB representation.  Infinity
is an irregular singular point after inversion `s=1/y`: the transformed
equation contains a pole of order three in its normal-form coefficient.  It
is already the irregular point at infinity of (1), not a gauge artifact.

The leading turning points are the roots of `P`.  Its discriminant is

\[
 \operatorname{disc}_yP=-4(8e^3+27).
\]

They are three distinct simple roots for generic `e`.  At

\[
 y_0=-1,\qquad e_0=-3/2,
\]

one has `P=P_y=0`, `P_yy=6`, and

\[
 P(y;-3/2)=-(y+1)^2(2y-1).
\]

Hence `y=-1` is a double turning point and `y=1/2` remains simple.  The other
two discriminant values are the complex rotations of this collision.  For
`e=e0+epsilon e1` and `y=-1+epsilon^(1/2)X`,

\[
 P=\varepsilon(2e_1+3X^2)+O(\varepsilon^{3/2}). \tag{7}
\]

The perturbations `epsilon Q1` and the energy detuning therefore split the
double point on the `epsilon^(1/2)` spatial scale.  A theorem for separated
simple turning points is not uniform in this limit.  One needs two
overlapping descriptions: ordinary Fedoryuk WKB/Airy domains away from the
pair and the close- or double-turning-point construction near it.  The
remaining simple point cannot be erased from a global continuation.

## Momentum, curves, and candidate domains

On a simply connected domain cut from the three roots and zero, choose the
branch

\[
 p(y,e)=\sqrt{Q_0(y,e)}=\frac{\sqrt{P(y;e)}}{y^2}
\]

by requiring `p(y,e)>0` for small positive real `y`.  This fixes the branch
near that interval; continuation fixes it elsewhere until a cut is crossed.
For a turning point `y_j`, define

\[
 S_j(y,e)=\int_{y_j}^{y}p(t,e)\,dt.
\]

If `y_j` has multiplicity `m` and

\[
 Q_0(y,e)=a_j(y-y_j)^m\{1+O(y-y_j)\},\qquad a_j\ne0,
\]

then, for a locally fixed square root,

\[
 S_j(y,e)\sim\frac{2\sqrt{a_j}}{m+2}
 (y-y_j)^{(m+2)/2}.
\]

Writing `theta=arg(y-y_j)` gives the analytic initial directions

\[
 \theta^{(S)}_k=\frac{(2k+1)\pi-\arg a_j}{m+2},\qquad
 \theta^{(A)}_k=\frac{2k\pi-\arg a_j}{m+2},
 \quad k=0,\ldots,m+1.                       \tag{8}
\]

Thus each family has `m+2` rays.  Reversing the square-root branch sends
`S_j` to `-S_j` and leaves both geometric zero-level sets unchanged.  At a
separated simple root, `a_j=partial_y Q_0(y_j,e)=P_y(y_j;e)/y_j^4` and (8)
gives three rays in each family.  At coalescence,

\[
 Q_0(y,-3/2)=3(y+1)^2+O((y+1)^3),
\]

so `a_0=3`.  Its Stokes directions are
`pi/4,3pi/4,5pi/4,7pi/4`, and its anti-Stokes directions are
`0,pi/2,pi,3pi/2`.  The remaining point `y=1/2` is simple with
`a=Q_0'(1/2)=-72`, again giving three plus three rays.

We use Fedoryuk's terminology: **Stokes curves** satisfy `Re S_j=0` and
carry oscillatory leading exponentials.  They are often called anti-Stokes
curves in physics.  Here **anti-Stokes curves** satisfy `Im S_j=0`.  A WKB
solution `Q0^(-1/4) exp(-S/epsilon)` is subdominant where `Re S>0`; the plus
solution is subdominant where `Re S<0`.

The figure `figures/fedoryuk_stokes_geometry.{pdf,png}` shows the exact
turning-point and pole locations and numerically integrated trajectories of
the leading quadratic differential for a nearby separated configuration and
at coalescence.  Every trajectory is launched on a direction (8).  It stops
at the pole neighbourhood, another turning-point neighbourhood, the plotting
boundary, or the maximum integration length; no stationary points are
appended after stopping and no curve is manually deleted.  Independent
midpoint quadrature of `p dy` along every displayed polyline gives maximum
forbidden-component variations `1.118e-4` (separated) and `1.129e-4`
(coalesced), below the tolerance `2e-4`.  The finite-radius starting
remainder is bounded by 32/64-point radial Gauss--Legendre comparison; its
maxima are `9.613e-5` and `9.997e-5`, below `2e-3`.  Initial incidence
directions are analytic, but continued curve shapes are numerical
illustrations, not a proof of global connectivity.

A Fedoryuk canonical domain is a simply connected component, with suitable
cuts, in which the phase is single-valued and every point can be joined to a
chosen boundary end by a path progressive for the selected sign of `Re S`.
Natural candidates are:

1. punctured sectors adjacent to `y=0`, excluding the pole;
2. ordinary WKB domains bounded by curves from the three simple roots when
   they are separated;
3. a close-turning-point neighbourhood containing the pair near `-1` but
   excluding zero and the remaining root;
4. outer domains reaching the subdominant sectors at infinity.

Near zero, `p~+y^(-2)`, so `S~-1/y`; the two exponentials are
`exp(plus_or_minus 1/(epsilon y))`.  Multiplication by the gauge in (2), for
which `eta/z=1/(epsilon y)`, cancels one sign and doubles the other.  Thus the
regular Bargmann germ is a compensated sectorial branch, not simply “the
decaying WKB solution.”  At infinity, `p~sqrt(-2/y)` and
`S~2sqrt(-2y)`, producing the already known order-one-half exponential
growth in `z`.  Both infinity branches are Bargmann admissible, so infinity
alone supplies no scalar spectral condition.

A candidate physical chain starts with the compensated branch in an origin
sector, enters a WKB domain, uses a double-turning-point connection near
`-1`, and continues through further WKB domains to the sheet-related origin
sector, with possible Airy passage at the remaining simple point.  No single
canonical domain can contain the pole or a turning point, and the displayed
graph does not prove that this chain avoids the remaining root.  If it does
not, an Airy connection is compulsory.  In either case a local Weber-type
connection for the close pair, plus overlap estimates and exact tracking of
the gauge monodromy, is compulsory.  These are precisely the missing global
steps.

## Fedoryuk source audit

The accessible table of contents of M. V. Fedoryuk, *Asymptotic Analysis:
Linear Ordinary Differential Equations* (Springer, 1993) verifies the
following locations.  The available preview did not expose theorem numbers
or the full theorem text; none is invented here.

The repository bibliography was also audited for Fedoryuk, Olver, Sibuya,
Evgrafov, and Voros.  It contains book entries for Fedoryuk and Olver, but no
entries for Sibuya, Evgrafov, or Voros.  Since no result of the latter authors
is invoked here, no unverified bibliographic item has been manufactured.

* Chapter III, “Second-Order Equations in the Complex Plane,” section 2,
  “WKB Bounds in the Complex Plane,” starts on p. 87.  This is the relevant
  source for exact solutions and error estimates on canonical/progressive
  domains away from zeros and poles.  The Kerr checks are: `Q0,Q1,Q2` are
  holomorphic after restricting to such a cut domain; `Q0` is nonzero there;
  a branch of `p` and its phase exists; and the domain must actually possess
  the required progressive paths.  The first three are local facts.  The
  fourth is established only for deliberately chosen sectorial domains, not
  for the proposed global chain.
* Chapter III, section 4, “Equations with Entire or Meromorphic
  Coefficients,” starts on p. 108.  This is essential because (5) is rational
  with a pole at zero.  Polynomial-potential results in section 3 do not
  apply globally.  Meromorphicity holds, but estimates cannot be continued
  through the pole.
* Chapter IV, “Second-Order Equations with Turning Points,” section 2, “A
  Simple Turning Point: The Complex Case,” starts on p. 182.  It applies
  locally at each separated simple root: the coefficient is analytic there,
  its derivative is nonzero, and a small disk can exclude all other roots
  and zero.  It is nonuniform when two roots approach one another.
* Chapter IV, section 4, “Multiple and Fractional Turning Points,” starts on
  p. 191, and section 6, “Multiple Turning Points: The Complex Case,” starts
  on p. 207.  They identify the relevant local class at the exact double
  root.  Their local hypotheses hold because `Q0` is analytic near `-1`, has
  a zero of exactly order two, and no pole there.
* The following section, “Two Close Turning Points,” starts on p. 211, and
  “Fusion of Several Turning Points” starts on p. 217.  The first is the
  relevant construction for `e-e0=O(epsilon)`, where the exact equation has
  two separated but coalescing roots.  It must be matched to ordinary domains
  because the root at `1/2` and the pole at zero remain outside the local
  neighbourhood.

The verified contents do not identify a standalone theorem that automatically
continues a solution through an arbitrary finite chain.  Global continuation
must therefore be assembled from overlapping canonical domains and local
connection matrices, with uniform error bounds on every overlap.  For Kerr,
the unverified hypotheses are: existence and topology of the entire physical
chain; progressive paths uniform in the close-pair scaling; control at the
pole endpoint; whether the simple point must be crossed; sheet and logarithm
monodromy; and reduction of all inadmissible origin multipliers to one scalar
condition.  They also prevent elevating the applicability framework to a
proved sectorial theorem and rule out Outcomes B and C.

## Comparison with the independent strong-drive expansion

This comparison is made only after the direct derivation above.  The leading
action is

\[
 \mathcal A(e)=\int_{y_-(e)}^{y_+(e)}\sqrt{P(y;e)/y^4}\,dy, \tag{9}
\]

on the close-pair momentum sheet and with a contour fixed by the eventual
physical connection problem.  Its vanishing at coalescence selects
`e0=-3/2`, and hence the leading energy `E/V=e eta^(4/3)`.

Equation (7) shows that the local connection depends on
`(e-e0)/epsilon`; therefore `e=e0+epsilon e1+...`.  Multiplication by the
leading energy scale gives

\[
 \eta^{4/3}\varepsilon=\eta^{2/3},\qquad
 \eta^{4/3}\varepsilon^2=1,\qquad
 \eta^{4/3}\varepsilon^3=\eta^{-2/3}.
\]

This independently explains the power ladder in the fixed-level expansion.
The index dependence can in principle be recovered from the close-pair
model connection coefficient.  The geometry independently checks the
leading `-3/2` and the spacing scale `eta^(2/3)`.  The explicit
`n`-dependent coefficient at that order, the constant term, and the
`eta^(-2/3)` coefficient are not consequences of the presently proved
Fedoryuk result: assigning a model zero to a Bargmann eigenvalue still needs
the missing global chain.  They remain independently verified formal
operator coefficients and consistency targets, not inputs to (4)--(8).

## Reproducibility and unresolved assumptions

`scripts/09a_verify_fedoryuk_normal_form.py` checks the gauge, exact removal
of the first derivative, all three coefficients in (5), relation (6), the
discriminant and double root, and every power counting above using exact
symbolic algebra.  `scripts/09a_fedoryuk_stokes_geometry.py` generates both
figure formats; numerical tracing is used only to display candidate curves.

The open mathematical problem is a finite pole--WKB--Weber--WKB (and, if
forced by topology, Airy) domain-chain lemma with uniform transition bounds,
full gauge/sheet monodromy, and a proof that its vanishing connection data are
equivalent to the entire Bargmann germ.  In particular no equality with the
previous Whittaker--Ince determinant is assumed.
